Sun & wind, over a year

Two places absorb the sun's heat at different rates through the year. That imbalance becomes a pressure gradient, and the Earth's rotation bends it into wind. Move through the year and change the two regions to see the pattern — and its reversal — emerge.

How this model works
  1. Insolation — each region's average daily solar energy per square metre, computed from its latitude and the sun's declination on that day (the same geometry behind sunrise times and day length).
  2. Temperature response — insolation swings through the year, but land and ocean don't heat and cool at the same rate. Land tracks the sun closely (short thermal lag); ocean responds slower and with a damped swing (long lag). This is modelled as a simple first-order thermal lag on the insolation signal.
  3. Temperature gradient — the difference between Region B and Region A's temperature signal on a given day.
  4. Wind — approximated as proportional to that gradient and inversely related to the Coriolis effect at the regions' latitude (a simplified geostrophic-style balance). This is also why the effect is exaggerated near the equator in the model (and floored there) and why the wind direction flips between seasons — the same basic mechanism behind monsoon reversal.

This is a teaching approximation, not a validated forecast model — real wind also depends on humidity, topography, ocean currents and more, none of which are included here.
